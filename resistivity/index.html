A contractor called us last spring about a proposed warehouse expansion near the Nechako River flats. The boreholes showed clay till but the groundwater readings were inconsistent across the site. We ran a VES array along three lines and identified a buried sand channel that was acting as a preferential drainage path. In Prince George, the glacial stratigraphy left by the Fraser Glaciation creates exactly these kinds of surprises. Our lab processes the resistivity data alongside physical samples to distinguish low-resistivity clays from saturated granular layers, which is critical when you are dealing with frost depth and ice lens potential. We usually pair the VES survey with a CPT test when the client needs continuous profiling in soft ground without bringing a drill rig onto sensitive terrain.

A resistivity line across glacial till and outwash in Prince George can change by 60 ohm-m in less than 20 meters. You cannot interpolate from a single borehole.

Methodology applied in Prince George

Around downtown Prince George, we often see resistivity contrasts that make textbook interpretation useless. The glacio-lacustrine silts near the river give readings between 15 and 40 ohm-m, but pockets of coarser outwash can jump to over 100 ohm-m within a few meters laterally. Our field crew uses a Schlumberger array with electrode spacings up to 200 meters to reach depths of 40 to 50 meters, which covers the typical investigation depth for multi-story foundations on the benchlands. The data inversion is done in-house using RES2DINV, and we ground-truth the results against logged test pits whenever possible. Before finalizing a foundation design north of the Hart Highway, we often recommend combining the resistivity profile with grain size analysis to confirm the fines content in zones where the resistivity signature is ambiguous.

Typical technical challenges in Prince George

The mistake we see repeatedly in Prince George is assuming that a low-resistivity anomaly is always groundwater. The clay-rich tills that blanket much of the bowl have intrinsic resistivity values that overlap with wet silty sand. A contractor who interprets a 25 ohm-m zone as a water table will install dewatering wells and pump dry clay forever. We have been called to fix this on three different commercial sites near the BCR Industrial Site. The correct approach is to cross-check the resistivity inversion with at least one direct push probe or a deep test pit so that stratigraphy, not just pore fluid, is confirmed. Another risk is ignoring seasonal frost effects: resistivity increases sharply when pore water freezes, and a survey run in February without temperature correction will misrepresent the true unfrozen soil conditions.

Our services

Our Prince George lab offers two tiers of electrical resistivity work, from single-site vertical soundings to multi-line imaging for larger developments. Every survey includes a site-specific interpretation report with 2D resistivity sections and correlation tables for local soil types.

Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES)

A Schlumberger array deployed at a single point to map resistivity changes with depth. We use this most often in Prince George for groundwater table mapping, depth-to-bedrock estimates on the benchlands, and identifying the base of the clay till layer before installing deep footings.

2D Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT)

A multi-electrode line that produces a continuous cross-section of subsurface resistivity. We apply ERT along proposed pipeline corridors, road widenings on Highway 16, and for landslide investigation on the cutbanks above the Fraser River where lateral variability is extreme.

Quick answers

What depth can a VES survey reach in Prince George soils?

With our standard 200-meter Schlumberger spread, we reach 40 to 50 meters in local silts and tills. The practical limit is set by the electrode spacing and the background noise from the electrical grid, but we have achieved 60-meter penetration on rural sites north of the city where cultural noise is low.

How much does a resistivity survey cost for a typical lot in Prince George?

A single VES sounding with interpretation runs between CA$810 and CA$1,540 depending on access conditions and the number of depth levels requested. A 2D ERT line adds cost for electrode deployment and longer inversion time. We provide a fixed quote after reviewing the site plan and access constraints.

Can you identify permafrost or ice lenses with resistivity?

Yes, frozen ground increases resistivity by an order of magnitude or more. In Prince George, discontinuous permafrost is not typical at surface, but we use resistivity to detect seasonal frost penetration and to map ice-rich lenses in the clay till that can cause differential heave under shallow foundations.
